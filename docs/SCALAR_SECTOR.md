# T3 scalar sector: operator basis, controlled reduction and numerical scope

**Authoritative scalar-sector explanation for the honours thesis.** This document consolidates the physics argument previously distributed across the scalar-relevance Batch 1–8 notes, the reduced-potential conclusion and the scalar-study separation notes. It describes the **ordinary split-scalar T3 models** in the project's 16-model catalogue. It does not assert that a fully general physical scalar-sector calculation has been implemented.

**Verification basis:** read-only inspection of repository commit `aa4dd2b7b6d2c31494e45290a88004822a8c43c5` (29 October 2026), including the scalar-audit scripts and existing documentation. Saved scientific artifacts are identified below. This documentation consolidation makes **no** change to the Lagrangian, beta functions, matching, numerical inputs or fitted results. ## 1. Physical objective and approximation

The calculation targets the leading, gauge-unbroken, dimension-five Majorana neutrino-mass operator generated by the **T3 one-loop topology** with two original Yukawa insertions and one scalar quartic insertion. The question answered by the scalar audit is:

> Which extra scalar operators can contribute directly to this specified fixed-order one-loop coefficient, and under what boundary conditions may the others be omitted from that calculation?

This is **not** a claim that the omitted operators are forbidden by gauge symmetry, irrelevant to the entire theory, or harmless at arbitrary nonzero input values.

The scalar fields `S1` and `S2` are **retained**. What is restricted is the collection of additional gauge- and Z2-allowed terms in the scalar **potential**. With model-specific SU(2) contractions understood,

\[
\begin{aligned}
V_{\mathrm{red}}={}&V_{\mathrm{SM}}+m_1^2 S_1^\dagger S_1+m_2^2 S_2^\dagger S_2
+V_{\mathrm{ordinary,quartic}}\\
&+\big[\lambda_{T3}(H H S_1 S_2^\dagger)_{\mathbf 1}+\mathrm{h.c.}\big].
\end{aligned}
\]

`V_ordinary,quartic` denotes the pre-existing Hermitian scalar self- and portal-interaction families in `Lagrangian/interactions/ScalarPotential.wl`. It includes the usual `S1`/`S2` self quartics, Higgs–scalar portals and scalar–scalar portals, with the representation-allowed invariant contractions. **These terms are retained even where they do not directly enter the selected C5 diagram**, because they affect the scalar RGEs, masses, vacuum properties and other observables.

The general operator basis adds extra quartics `O_i^(4)` and quadratic bilinears `O_a^(2)` with independent coefficients. The reduced benchmark specifies

\[
\boxed{\lambda_{\mathrm{extra},i}(\Lambda)=0,\qquad
\mu^2_{\mathrm{extra},a}(\Lambda)=0.}
\]

This is an **input boundary condition**, not a new symmetry. The scale `Lambda` must be stated for each numerical scenario: `muF = 10^5 GeV` in the two **UV-frozen** reduced-scalar scenarios; `muUV = 10^7 GeV` in the **UV-running** scenario. An earlier restricted B/C UV-running pilot also used `10^7 GeV`; it must not be confused with the frozen cases.

The corresponding one-loop vertex analysis assumes **diagonal complex-scalar propagators at tree level**, no extra mass mixing, the electroweak-unbroken theory, a single scalar quartic insertion, and the leading dimension-five Weinberg projection. Additional Higgs VEV insertions, dimension-seven operators, nontrivial scalar mass rotations, extra loops and arbitrary sequential thresholds are outside that vertex test.

## 3. Direct leading one-loop C5 selection

Schematically, the source topology uses

\[
y_1\,\overline L F^c S_1,\qquad
 y_2\,\overline L F S_2^\dagger,
\]

with exact charge conjugations and SU(2) contractions fixed by `Lagrangian/interactions/T3Topology.wl`. The selected lepton-side Yukawa vertices provide scalar legs `S1` and/or `S2dag`. With diagonal free scalar propagators,

\[
\langle S_1 S_1^\dagger\rangle\ne 0,\qquad
\langle S_2 S_2^\dagger\rangle\ne 0,
\]

so the quartic at the other end must contain the conjugate scalar legs. Its permitted scalar-end combinations in the Wick screening are:

| Quartic scalar legs | Yukawa pairing |
|---|---|
| `(S1dag)^2` | `y1 y1` |
| `S1dag S2` | `y1 y2` |
| `S2^2` | `y2 y2` |

The holomorphic Weinberg projection used by the audit requires the **`Hdag Hdag` orientation** at that vertex, calibrated against the Hermitian conjugate of the original `HH S1 S2dag` interaction. Both orientations of a complex quartic are tested.

The exact extra-quartic screening result is:

| Classification across 16 models | Slots | Direct single-quartic-vertex contribution to the selected C5 graph |
|---|---:|---|
| Four BSM scalar legs, no Higgs legs | 26 | Cannot supply both external Higgs legs |
| `H Hdag` with the wrong Weinberg projection orientation | 10 | Excluded for this holomorphic one-vertex projection |
| Correct Higgs orientation, incompatible scalar Wick ends | 10 | Excluded with diagonal scalar propagators |
| Correct Higgs and scalar leg structure | **4** | Wick-eligible; observed separately in saved symbolic C5 |
| **Total** | **50** | **46 excluded from this direct graph, 4 eligible** |

The four eligible extra quartic **model-slots** are two each in **B(-1)** and **C(-1)**:

| Matchete extra coupling | Schematic conjugate-oriented operator | Pairing |
|---|---|---|
| `lambdaT3ExtraQ020020I1` | `(Hdag)^2 S2^2` | `y2 y2` |
| `lambdaT3ExtraQ020200I1` | `(Hdag)^2 (S1dag)^2` | `y1 y1` |

These appear in the saved symbolic one-loop C5 artifacts **if their coefficients are independently nonzero**. They are **not** intrinsically two-loop operators. The `lambdaT3` vertex remains the original direct source for all 16 models, with the `y1 y2` pairing.

The Wick test establishes **necessary field-flow conditions**, not a complete amplitude proof: spinor contractions, Majorana conventions, CG tensors and normalization can still determine whether an eligible amplitude vanishes or its coefficient. The four eligible extra terms are supported separately by their occurrence in saved symbolic matching results. The 46 exclusions do **not** imply that these operators are absent from the full potential or unable to influence RG running, scalar spectra, vacuum stability, electroweak breaking or other Feynman diagrams.

## 4. Additional quadratic interactions: a distinct assumption

Eight models admit one additional complex bilinear under the project symmetries:

| Model assignments | Allowed extra bilinear (schematic) |
|---|---|
| A(0), D(0), E(0) | `(S1dag)^2 + h.c.` |
| A(-2), D(-2), E(-2) | `(S2dag)^2 + h.c.` |
| B(-1), C(-1) | `epsilon(S1dag, S2dag) + h.c.` |

With the quadratic potential convention used in the candidate spectrum audit,

\[
V_2=m_1^2S_1^\dagger S_1+m_2^2S_2^\dagger S_2+
\big[\mu_2\,{\cal I}_2+\mathrm{h.c.}\big],
\]

there is **no implicit factor of 1/2** on the last term. For self-bilinears, the squared-mass eigenvalues are

\[
m_{\pm}^2=m_{\mathrm{self}}^2\pm 2|\mu_2|.
\]

For the B/C doublet-mixing candidate, rewriting one field in the conjugate representation gives

\[
M_\Phi^2=\begin{pmatrix}m_1^2&\mu_2\\\mu_2^*&m_2^2\end{pmatrix},\quad
m_\pm^2=\frac{m_1^2+m_2^2}{2}\pm
\sqrt{\left(\frac{m_1^2-m_2^2}{2}\right)^2+|\mu_2|^2},
\]

\[
|\sin2\theta|=\frac{2|\mu_2|}{\sqrt{(m_1^2-m_2^2)^2+4|\mu_2|^2}}.
\]

**A nonzero bilinear modifies propagators or scalar mixing at tree level.** Its insertion into a neutrino-mass diagram requires no further loop suppression and can alter the leading one-loop C5. Near degenerate masses, mixing may be appreciable even if mass-eigenvalue shifts are small. The eight bilinears therefore cannot be rejected by the quartic Wick criterion or by generic loop-counting. The reduced model sets them to zero as a separate boundary choice and uses diagonal propagators.

The saved candidate spectrum comparison **did not** calculate a general nonzero-mixing C5. It compared the analytic scalar eigenmasses at fixed diagonal squared masses; it was not a re-integrated alternative RGE trajectory or a quantitative neutrino-mass error test.

**Sources:** `scripts/AuditT3QuadraticImpact.py`, `output/scalar_relevance/quadratic_impact_audit.json` and the saved candidate quadratic EFT1 reports.

## 5. Renormalisation-group generation and perturbative power counting

Use the project's one-loop convention

\[
16\pi^2\frac{dX}{d\ln\mu}=\beta_X^{(1)}.
\]

With `lambda_extra(Lambda)=0`, a nonzero source independent of that extra coupling generates at leading logarithmic order

\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{extra}}(\mu)\simeq
\frac{\beta_{\lambda,0}^{(1)}}{16\pi^2}\ln\!\frac{\mu}{\Lambda}.
\]

Inserting this **radiatively generated** coupling into a **one-loop** C5 graph gives schematically

\[
\boxed{\Delta C_5^{\mathrm{generated\ quartic}}\sim
\frac{\ln(\mu/\Lambda)}{(16\pi^2)^2}\,\mathcal K
\big(\beta_{\lambda,0}^{(1)},y_1,y_2,M_F,m_{S_i}\big).}
\]

It has two *formal fixed-order* loop factors: one for RG generation and one for C5 matching. This is the conditional justification for omitting the **generated direct B/C extra-quartic insertion** from a strict leading fixed-one-loop C5. It does **not** mean a coupling independently nonzero at the UV boundary is higher order.

For a scale ratio of 100,

\[
\frac{\ln 100}{16\pi^2}\simeq 0.0292.
\]

This is an expansion factor **before** actual beta coefficients, tensor factors, Yukawas, interference, large-log resummation and possible cancellations. **It is not a 2.9% bound on neutrino masses or mixing angles.**

### 5.1 What the saved candidate beta-function audits found

- Across the 16 models, **6 additional quartic slots** (3 in B(-1) and 3 in C(-1)) have an immediate first-order UV one-loop source at the all-extra-zero boundary. **4 of these** are the two-per-model direct C5 candidates.
- The saved candidate **EFT1** beta source test found **0** immediately sourced extra quartics at the local zero-extra boundary. This does **not** erase quartics already generated in the UV and transported into EFT1.
- Each of the **8 extra quadratic coefficients** has a possible one-loop mass-beta source proportional to additional quartic couplings when those quartics are independently nonzero. At zero extra quadratic **and** zero extra quartics, that direct mass-beta source vanishes.
- The iterated one-loop dependency analysis, applied **separately** to the saved candidate UV and EFT1 systems, did **not find a finite source chain** reaching the extra quadratic slots from the all-extra-zero boundary. This is a statement about those exported beta expressions and the tested source graph, **not** a non-generation theorem for physical thresholds, two-loop beta functions or a general EFT.

The audit used the formal recurrence

\[
\operatorname{ord}(X_i)=1+
\min_{m\subset\beta_i^{(1)}}\sum_{X_j\in m}\operatorname{ord}(X_j),
\]

where retained couplings have insertion order zero and extra couplings begin without an assigned finite order. This orders *iterated one-loop beta sources*, not independent physical diagrams.

The mass-beta audit explicitly found, for example, a candidate B/C mixed-bilinear source of the form

\[
\left.\beta_{\mu_{12}^2}^{(1)}\right|_{\mu_{12}^2=0}
=6\lambda_{S_1S_1^{\dagger2}S_2^\dagger}\,m_{S_1}^2+
 6\lambda_{S_1^\dagger S_2S_2^{\dagger2}}\,m_{S_2}^2,
\]

with saved RGBeta symbols `lambdaS1S1bar2S2bar` and `lambdaS1barS2S2bar2`. Hence **zero bilinears alone are not a generally RG-closed restriction** when extra quartics are nonzero.

## 6. What was actually calculated numerically

### 6.1 Conditional B/C zero-boundary pilot (not final physical general matching)

The earlier `scripts/RunT3ZeroExtraBoundaryBenchmark.py` study examined **B(-1) and C(-1)** with all seven model-specific additional quartics and the one model-specific extra quadratic exactly zero at `muUV=10^7 GeV`; candidate one-loop UV and EFT1 RGBeta equations were integrated across `10^7 -> 10^5 -> 10^3 GeV`. The optional *frozen one-scale* C5 preview numerically **identified RGBeta and Matchete quartics by assumption** and used frozen Yukawa/diagonal scalar masses. The associated numerical response is **not a certified C5 uncertainty or neutrino-mass prediction**. The follow-up low-energy continuation reused the existing model-specific C5 rather than calculating a complete matching with the additional quartics.

Relevant outputs: `output/scalar_relevance/zero_boundary_benchmark/` and `output/scalar_relevance/zero_boundary_low_energy/`.

### 6.2 Final reduced-potential frozen-UV fits (primary thesis results)

The completed reduced study uses **16 models x 2 UV-frozen scenarios** with `lambdaT3=5e-9` and `0.5` at `muF=10^5 GeV` for A–D (Class E uses half these inputs), diagonal scalar benchmark masses in the **0.5–5 TeV** window and the retained heavy-fermion hierarchy screen `rhoF <= 0.5`.

- **32/32** individually optimised Yukawa fits satisfied the specified five-observable screening criterion (`chi2 <= 5.86`).
- **10/32** evaluations of the scenario-matched A(-2) reference Yukawas in other model backgrounds passed that same screen.
- The fits used **18 real Yukawa parameters** for five target observables. They demonstrate conditional fit viability, **not** prediction of the fitted mixing angles or unique parameter determination.
- Additional quartic sidecars are zero at the applicable input boundary and are evolved where supported by **candidate** native one-loop RGEs. The additional bilinears are zero and are **not evolved** in this final reduced-scalar campaign, under the conditional source-audit argument. Radiatively generated direct extra B/C quartics are **not inserted** into the fixed-order one-loop C5.
- The model-specific C5 evaluation is hierarchical for **12 models**; **4 extreme-hypercharge cases** retain legacy one-scale matching. Complete general-scalar sequential Wilson matching and a physically certified Matchete/RGBeta map are **not implemented**.

Source campaign: `output/studies/reduced_scalar_sobol/reduced_frozen_overnight_v2/`.
Final reporting: `output/studies/reduced_scalar_final_reports/final_reduced_v2/`.
Authoritative campaign/report scripts: `scripts/RunT3ReducedScalarSobolComparison.py`, `scripts/BuildT3ReducedScalarFinalResults.py`.

A separate historical UV-running diagnostic from `10^7 -> 10^5 GeV` found that its chosen scalar masses can leave the **0.5–5 TeV** benchmark region, making the nominal scalar EFT1 endpoint unsuitable for a physically rematched low-energy neutrino prediction in those cases. This does **not** exclude all UV-running T3 boundaries.

### 6.3 Exploratory nonzero-additional-quartic campaigns

The `general_scalar_extended` / general-scalar-48 studies evolve some additional quartics under candidate RGEs. They do **not** establish fully physical general-scalar C5 matching across all 16 models, arbitrary scalar bilinears, a certified native Matchete/RGBeta coupling map, or complete sequential EFT Wilson evolution. Their fitted angle agreement after Yukawa re-optimisation cannot be used to bound the effect of omitted scalar terms at *fixed Yukawas*.

Keep exploratory results in their own output tree; **do not replace** the final reduced-potential fit data with these campaigns or merge their acceptance counts.

## 7. Status of the justification: what can and cannot be claimed

| Statement | Status |
|---|---|
| Gauge/Z2-invariant additional scalar-operator slots were inventoried across 16 models | Verified in saved manifest/audit |
| 46 extra quartic slots cannot independently furnish the specified one-quartic-vertex unbroken LLSS-to-LLHH diagram | Verified **within diagonal propagator/field-flow scope** |
| Four B/C(-1) extra quartic slots can enter saved one-loop C5 if independently nonzero | Supported by saved symbolic matching observations |
| Generated extra quartics are formally higher fixed order when inserted into one-loop C5 from an all-zero UV input | Perturbative deduction **conditional on boundary and source assumptions** |
| Eight permitted extra bilinears are generally negligible at one loop | **Not established**; nonzero tree bilinears may modify leading C5 |
| No extra quadratic generated in the separately tested candidate one-loop dependency graphs | Supported by saved audit; **not** a general threshold/higher-loop theorem |
| Matchete/RGBeta general quartic normalization and full sequential general-scalar matching are certified | **Not established** |
| Mixing-angle stability after refitting measures fixed-input scalar corrections | **False**; fitted observables are conditioned on the fit |
| The scalar potential is the most general renormalisable theory | **False**; it is an explicit restricted benchmark |

The accurate thesis formulation is **"a systematically justified restricted scalar-sector benchmark for leading-dimension-five, fixed-order one-loop C5, under vanishing additional-coupling boundary conditions and diagonal tree-level scalar propagators"**, not "the general scalar potential was shown irrelevant".

### Suggested thesis wording

> We constructed an inventory of the additional gauge- and Z2-invariant scalar interactions permitted in the ordinary T3 models and classified their relevance to the leading one-loop Weinberg-operator matching. Of 50 additional quartic coupling slots across 16 model assignments, 46 cannot contribute as the sole quartic insertion in the selected unbroken-phase topology with diagonal scalar propagators, while four B(-1)/C(-1) slots are allowed and appear in the saved symbolic matching. In the reduced benchmark, all additional quartic and quadratic coefficients vanish at the specified input boundary; RG generation of the direct extra quartics therefore places their insertion into the one-loop amplitude beyond strict leading fixed order. Extra quadratic interactions require a separate zero-boundary assumption because nonzero coefficients can alter scalar propagation at tree level. Thus the numerical study employs a controlled, explicitly restricted scalar potential rather than claiming a fully general scalar-sector prediction.
